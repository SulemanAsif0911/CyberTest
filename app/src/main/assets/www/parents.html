For parents and teachers

Respond with calm, not blame.

How you react in the first minutes shapes whether a young person asks for help again. These steps are written for parents, carers, teachers and counsellors.

The most important sentence“Thank you for telling me. You’re not in trouble. We’ll sort this out together.”

What to say, and what to avoid

Say and do

  • Stay calm and listen first. Let them tell it their way.
  • Tell them it isn’t their fault, whatever they sent or did.
  • Ask what they need right now.
  • Save evidence together, then report together.
  • Keep it private, and agree who else needs to know.
  • Check in again in a day or two.

Avoid

  • Blaming: “Why did you send it?”
  • Taking their phone or banning apps as punishment.
  • Confronting the other person yourself.
  • Deleting messages or accounts before saving evidence.
  • Sharing the images with others, even to “show” someone.
  • Promising it will be fixed immediately.

Signs a child may be in trouble

Warning signs
AreaWhat you may notice
MoodSudden sadness, anxiety, anger or withdrawal.
Phone useHiding the screen, panic at notifications, or suddenly avoiding the phone.
SchoolSkipping classes, falling grades, avoiding friends.
Sleep and bodyTrouble sleeping, headaches, stomach aches, changes in appetite.
MoneyAsking for money, or unexplained payments or gift cards.

Steps for a family or school

  1. Save evidence with the child’s help in the vault. Include usernames, links, dates and the full conversation.
  2. Secure accounts: new passwords, two-step verification, and review who can contact them.
  3. Report to the platform (guides) and to NCCIA 1799 or FIA 9911.
  4. Involve the school if classmates are involved. Ask for its anti-bullying or harassment policy and a named contact.
  5. Support wellbeing: offer a counsellor or a trusted adult, and watch for signs they feel hopeless.
Intimate images of a childDon’t forward or keep copies on your own phone beyond what is needed as evidence. Use Take It Down to help remove them, and report promptly.
